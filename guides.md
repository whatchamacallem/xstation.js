# House Guests: 50 Guided Experiences, Ranked by Mass Audience Appeal

## Rules every experience follows

1. **It was happening anyway.** The guide does this for a living or was about to do it today. The player takes an empty place: the second paddle, the fourth astronomer, the spare horse.
2. **The player does the job.** Each experience has several hands-on tasks and one set piece, with real rules and a real chance of failing.
3. **Something of the guide's is at stake.** A herd, a rent payment, a record, a deadline. The player's choices change how it ends.
4. **The exit promised in the introduction always works.** No guide overrides the player's feelings for the sake of drama.
5. **One sitting, one ending, one keepsake.** Each is a complete paid session that ends with something personal to keep and share.
6. **The world remembers.** A calf that grows up, a bowl set in a wall, a street of neighbors.

## Policy and rating flags

These are flags to raise with counsel and platform reviewers, not legal advice.

1. **Companion chatbot law.** If the guides are AI-driven, California's SB 243 (in force since 1 January 2026) exempts a bot that is a feature of a video game only while its replies stay on the game and it cannot discuss mental health, self-harm or sexually explicit conduct, or hold conversations on unrelated topics. Keep every guide inside its own world. Idris is the risk: the player's open question has to be answered in-world by the scholars of Baghdad, with a refusal path for those topics.
2. **Age rating.** Alcohol appears as period detail (honeyed wine, mare's milk, illegal punch), and original introduction 19 offers coca leaves. Never make them a mechanic or a reward, and expect an alcohol and drug reference descriptor.
3. **Simulated gambling.** Dice, backgammon and wagers on races stay inside the story. Do not attach purchasable currency to chance outcomes, which brings gambling and loot box rules into play.
4. **Real people.** Every guide is invented. Keep real figures offstage and unnamed, as the introductions already do: the two balloonists, the prince, the king, the speaker at midnight in Accra, the rocket engineers, the border guards. Three settings (1957, 1958 and 1989) are within living memory, so put no invented words in a real person's mouth.
5. **Living cultures.** Many guides stand in living traditions, among them Velan, Mahina, Sira, Waabigwan, Ki Sastro, Risten, Sun-deok and Dawa. Budget for paid cultural review, keep the player's own creations secular (the lamp, not the goddess), and review generated keepsakes before release.
6. **Hard history.** The trading voyages (Bakari, Aaltje) and the caravan market (Garba) sit inside economies that included slavery and colonial violence, and the Blitz and the Wall involve real deaths. Never make people a trade good, and keep the tone respectful.
7. **Music and text rights.** Use original or public-domain music for the cattle drive, the tango, the rent party, the highlife band and the truck radio. Radio hits of the 1970s are in copyright. Write an original telling of the Mali epic instead of adapting a published translation. The film, show and game comparisons in this document are for internal positioning and should stay out of marketing.
8. **Player likeness and children.** Portrait keepsakes (the glass-plate portrait, the shadow puppet, the picture with the Earth) need explicit consent if they use a player's photo, and biometric privacy laws may apply. Do not process children's images or data without verified parental consent.
9. **Physical and medical safety.** The diving game must run on timing and never ask for real breath-holding. Aurelia's remedies are history, not advice, and must be framed that way.

## The 50 guides ranked

### 1. Isaiah Cole: Two Thousand Head to Dodge

*Western trail adventure. Appeal score 92 (demand 10, hook 9, play 9, return 8). Original introduction 32.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> A campfire, and past its edge more stars than seem reasonable. Out in the dark a great many large animals are breathing. Isaiah Cole sits on his heels, a long, quiet man in a hat that has been rained on for years. He holds out a tin cup. "Coffee. It's bad, but it's hot. Two thousand head, bound for Dodge City. Tonight all I do is ride around them slow and sing, so they know nothing's sneaking up. They aren't particular about the singing. You don't have to say a word. There's a gentle horse saddled, if you'd like to see the sky from out there."

**Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Ride the last hundred miles of a cattle drive to Dodge City: sing the herd quiet on night guard, turn a stampede in a lightning storm, swim the river, and bring every head in.

**What the player does.** Isaiah has ridden night guard every night since Texas, and tonight there is a saddled horse for you. The first hours are slow circles under the stars, singing whatever you know, since the cattle only need to hear a voice. He teaches you to read the herd the way he does, by its breathing. Then the weather comes. When lightning puts two thousand animals on their feet, you ride for the front and help bend the run into a circle before it reaches broken ground. At sunrise forty head are missing, and you track them. There is still a river to swim and a hard outfit at the ford demanding a toll in cattle. Dodge City means the count at the stockyard gate, the pay, and a bath.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** A new trail name, and the start of a cowboy RPG. Next season is a whole new trail.

**Why it sells.** The cowboy fantasy behind some of the best-selling games and most-watched dramas of the last decade, with the quiet companion every rider wants.

### 2. Ruth Calloway: The First Ride's Free

*Flight and stunt adventure. Appeal score 92 (demand 9, hook 10, play 9, return 9). Original introduction 38.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> Flat bright daylight, a hayfield mown short, gasoline and warm varnish. A biplane stands a few yards off, its wings patched in three shades of cream. Ruth Calloway drops off the lower wing in boots and breeches, goggles pushed up into red hair. "Morning! Five dollars buys ten minutes, but you slept through my pitch, so the first ride's free. And before you ask about the wires, I checked every one myself. I'm bold. I'm not stupid. Front seat's yours. I'll take her up easy, and if you hate it, tap your head twice and I'll set us down like a feather. No questions, no teasing."

**Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Go barnstorming across 1920s America in an open-cockpit biplane: take the stick, loop over a county fair, walk the wing if you dare, and land in a hayfield when the engine quits.

**What the player does.** Ruth works her way from town to town all summer selling rides, and she is short a partner. The first flight is gentle, as promised, and by the end of it you have the stick. She teaches you turns, then a loop, then a roll, and each one is a sequence you fly yourself. Over the next town you buzz Main Street and throw out handbills, then spend the afternoon selling rides to cover fuel. The fair wants a show. That means a race against a motorcar down the county road, and the wing walk, which is always optional. Somewhere over open country the engine coughs and stops. You pick the field, judge the wind, and bring her in. Tap your head twice at any point and Ruth sets down like a feather.

The simulation has to be turn based and use a real world aviation stunt model for gameplay mechanics.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** A handbill with your name beside hers. Every visit is a new town and a new stunt, building toward the air races.

**Why it sells.** Flying is the oldest wish there is, and flight games have a huge following. Ruth is the instructor who makes it feel possible, and her tap-out rule lets nervous customers say yes.

### 3. Risten: The Herd Goes to the Sea

*Arctic journey, cozy survival. Appeal score 90 (demand 10, hook 9, play 7, return 9). Original introduction 33.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> Blue snow. Then a green curtain unrolls silently across the sky, and beneath it stands a tent with firelight at its seams. Bells clank where a herd is bedded down. Risten ducks out, a compact woman in a blue tunic banded with red, and stands beside your couch looking up before she says anything. "It does that. You get used to it. You do not get tired of it." Her breath smokes. "Come in by the fire when you are ready. No hurry. In the morning the herd moves to the coast, and there is room on my sled for one more."

**Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Drive a reindeer sled under the northern lights on the spring migration to the coast, and keep the herd together across river ice, a whiteout, and the tracks of something following the calves.

**What the player does.** Risten's family takes the herd to the coast every spring, and there is room on her sled. The first night asks nothing of you but looking up. In the morning you learn to harness, to drive, and to throw a lasso badly. The herd follows one belled animal, and your work is keeping the stragglers with her. The river has to be crossed while the ice still holds, and you help choose where. On the high ground a whiteout closes in, and Risten shows you how to hold a line by the wind on your cheek. Wolverine tracks have shadowed the herd for two days, so you take a turn on night watch with the dogs. A calf is born early, far from shelter, and rides the rest of the way on your sled. The journey ends where the snow stops and the sea begins.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** Your own earmark, the cut pattern that says whose reindeer is whose, and the calf that carries it. Come back for the autumn gathering and find that calf grown.

**Why it sells.** Seeing the northern lights comes first in survey after survey of bucket-list wishes, and reindeer sledding under them is already sold as a premium winter tour.

### 4. Dawa: Nobody Left Behind

*Mountaineering survival. Appeal score 90 (demand 9, hook 10, play 9, return 8). Original introduction 40.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> Pink on white, very high up: the first sun finding a wall of snow while everything below is blue. A rattle of flags, the hiss of a stove. Dawa squats beside it, a sturdy man with a sunburned nose, and holds up a tin mug. "Tea, with butter and salt. You will hate it for two days, then ask for more." He looks you over the way he would check a rope. "Head hurts? Tell me if it starts. On the mountain nobody is brave about headaches. The sahibs want the summit. I like the walking. I go slowly, and I have never left anyone behind."

**Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Climb toward the summit of Everest on a rope with the Sherpa who has never left anyone behind, and face the decision every climber dreads: go on, or turn around.

**What the player does.** Dawa has carried for expeditions most of his life, and you join his rope as the newest climber. The mountain is taken in stages, and each has its own test: crevasses crossed on the glacier before the sun loosens the ice, loads carried to the high camps, nights when breathing is work. He asks about your head every morning, and telling the truth is part of the game, because a hidden headache costs more later. You choose what to carry, when to rest, and whether the weather window is real. High on the ridge a member of the party goes down, and the summit and the rescue cannot both happen. Dawa will follow your call. He already knows which one lets him keep his record.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** A photograph from your high point and a white scarf from Dawa. Return for a different route, or for the summit you turned away from.

**Why it sells.** Everest is the most famous mountain on Earth, the trek to its base camp is a top-ten bucket-list trip, and push on or turn back is the survival genre's defining choice.

### 5. Dot Kemp: London by Ear

*Blitz survival and rescue. Appeal score 88 (demand 9, hook 9, play 9, return 8). Original introduction 41.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> The dark stays dark, but it changes: a slot of dimmed torchlight on wet pavement, paper tape crossed on windows, searchlights leaning about the sky. A siren is winding down. Dot Kemp crouches by your couch in a tin hat marked W, a brisk woman with a flask under her arm. "Hello, love. You're all right. That's the all clear." Her torch stays pointed at the ground, never at your face. "I'm warden for this street. I know every cellar, and I've walked many people through the black who didn't fancy it. Tea first. Then take my elbow, and I'll show you London by ear."

**Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Walk one night of the London Blitz beside an air-raid warden: get a street into shelter, put out a firebomb with a bucket pump, and find a family in a fallen house by listening.

**What the player does.** Dot walks this street every night of the raids, and she starts you on the quiet part: checking the blackout, learning who sleeps in which cellar. That knowledge matters within the hour. When the siren goes you bring the street in, including the old man who will not leave his dog. Firebombs land on the roofs, and you work the pump while she takes the hose. Then a house comes down. You carry the report to the post, word for word, and come back to help the rescue men, who call for silence and listen. A tap on a pipe, a voice, a dog: you tell them where to dig. The all clear comes at dawn, with tea from a van and the street still standing, mostly.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** Dot's incident report with your name in it and a tin hat marked with your initial. Each visit is a different night on the same street, and the neighbors remember you.

**Why it sells.** The World Wars come first in UK polling of favorite historical eras, and this is the bravest civilian story in them, with no weapon in anyone's hand.

### 6. Sigrid: Room at the Oars

*Viking voyage, survival and tactics. Appeal score 88 (demand 9, hook 9, play 9, return 8). Original introduction 8.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> A long fire catches down the middle of the dark and finds a hall of timber and smoke, snow light at the roof hole. Stone weights knock where Sigrid works a standing loom, a tall woman with gray braids and keys at her belt. "Come nearer the fire if you want," she says without turning. Then she does turn, holding up a width of wool. "This is a sail. Three winters of spinning, mine and my daughters'. In spring it goes to sea. I am tired of weaving them and waving from the shore. This time I go with it. There is room at the oars."

**Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Take an oar on a Viking ship's spring crossing of the open North Sea, under the sail Sigrid spent three winters weaving.

**What the player does.** Every spring the ship goes west with wool, furs and whetstones to trade, and this year Sigrid goes with it. You take the bench beside hers. You learn the stroke, the steering oar, and how to hold a course by the sun's shadow, the color of the water and the flight of birds. On the second night the weather turns and the crew has to shorten sail in the dark. It is her sail, and nobody yet knows if it will hold. Then comes fog, and a strange ship moving in it. Run, talk or raise shields is the crew's decision, and you have a vote and a place in the line. On the far shore the cargo is weighed out against silver, and you bargain for her share.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** A verse about your voyage in the old skaldic manner, with your name in it. Later sailings go farther: the islands, Ireland, Iceland.

**Why it sells.** Vikings are one of the surest draws in games and television, and Viking survival is a proven hit. This one adds a heroine the genre rarely offers.

### 7. Hori: Walking the Twelve Gates

*Tomb puzzle adventure. Appeal score 86 (demand 9, hook 9, play 8, return 8). Original introduction 3.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> Morning arrives all at once, white and dry, and the wall behind your couch is suddenly fresh plaster ruled with red guidelines. Hori steps back from it with a reed brush in each hand, a wiry man of fifty with blue ground into his knuckles. "Hold still," he says, and laughs. "Forgive me. **Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Go down into a pharaoh's unfinished tomb by lamplight and walk the road his soul must take, gate by gate, before the door is sealed for eternity.

**What the player does.** Hori's crew crosses the ridge to the royal valley every working day, and no tomb is closed until its painters have walked it end to end to prove the king's road to the afterlife has no mistakes. You draw your ration of lamp wicks, climb the path with the crew, and paint one star of your own on the blue ceiling. Then the walk begins. There are twelve painted gates, each with a guardian whose name must be read correctly from the wall, and somewhere among them is one sign an apprentice got wrong. Your lamp burns down the whole time. Deep inside, Hori stops at fresh chisel marks that no workman of his made. **Keepsake and next purchase.** Your star stays on the ceiling, and your name is hidden in hieroglyphs where only the dead will read it. Each new tomb has a new layout and a new set of gates.

**Why it sells.** Egyptian tombs are a staple of escape rooms and museum blockbusters, and this one is led by the man who painted it.

### 8. Noor Haddad: The Garden on the Rock

*Space survival and farming sim. Appeal score 84 (demand 9, hook 8, play 8, return 8). Original introduction 48.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> Violet light rises, the glow of growing lamps, falling on leaves: row after row in white troughs. Beyond them a window holds a black sky and a blue Earth small enough to cover with a thumb. **Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Work a shift in the greenhouse that feeds 200 people on the Moon: learn to move in one-sixth gravity, bring in the harvest, and save the crop when a solar storm and a failed pump arrive together.

**What the player does.** Noor tends this garden every day, and once the tomato is eaten she puts you to work. You learn to walk without bouncing off the ceiling, pollinate by hand because there are no bees, and balance the feed for the troughs. You help choose the next planting, weighing calories against the strawberries everyone begs for. Then two alarms sound. A solar storm is coming, with thirty minutes to reach shelter, and the pump that keeps the seedlings alive has stopped. There is time to save some of it. You choose what, carry trays to the shielded vault in long low strides, and go outside in a suit to turn the one valve that only opens by hand, with the Earth hanging over the crater rim. You ride out the storm with next season's seedlings on your knees.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** A variety in the seed vault named by you, and a picture of you with the Earth behind. Come back to see your planting grown and to face the next season's problem.

**Why it sells.** Farming simulators and survival games are two of the steadiest sellers on PC, space travel is a standing bucket-list wish, and the survive-by-gardening story has already carried a hit film.

### 9. Oharu: Past the Hakone Gate

*Deduction and stealth adventure. Appeal score 82 (demand 9, hook 7, play 8, return 8). Original introduction 27.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> Rain on thatch, and a paper lantern blooming amber beside a doorway. Around it a mountain road assembles in the dusk, and your couch sits under the eaves of a teahouse like one of its benches. **Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Spend a rainy night at a teahouse on the shogun's great road, learn to read every traveler from the feet up, and help one of them slip past the checkpoint before dawn.

**What the player does.** Reading the road is what Oharu does all day, and she teaches you while you pour. The barrier ahead searches every woman leaving the capital. Oharu knows a path over the mountain that the guards do not watch. You carry the lantern, choose when to cover it, and get the traveler past patrols, a river and her own nerves. By sunrise Oharu is back at her kettle as if nothing happened.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** A woodblock-style print of the teahouse in the rain with you on the bench. Every night the road sends different travelers and a different trouble.

**Why it sells.** Japan tops recent dream-destination surveys and samurai-era drama is a proven draw on screen. This gets a player inside that world without a sword.

### 10. Mahina: The Island Answering

*Ocean voyage and navigation survival. Appeal score 82 (demand 8, hook 8, play 8, return 9). Original introduction 13.*

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> The floor begins to move, and you know it for water before you see any. Stars come out overhead. Your couch is lashed between two hulls, under a sail of woven leaf. Mahina sits at the steering paddle, a lean woman with salt dried white on her shoulders. "Feel that? The big swell from the east, and a small one crossing it. The small one is an island answering." She lifts her chin toward a star clearing the sea. "We follow her until she climbs too high, then the next. You need to know nothing yet. Only like the sky, and tell me when you are tired."

**Guided experience**

**Pitch.** Find an island none of the crew has ever seen, steering a double-hulled canoe across open ocean by stars, swells and birds, with no instruments at all.

**What the player does.** Mahina is a navigator in the middle of a voyage, and you have simply woken up on it. She starts you with one job, holding the canoe on a rising star, and adds another each watch: feeling which swell is which through the hull, trimming the sail, catching rain. A squall takes the stars away for a night, and you steer by the waves alone. The water runs low. Then the signs begin, and you learn to read them before she tells you: birds flying out at dawn and home at dusk, a cloud that stands still, a green tint beneath it. The crew looks to the steering paddle for the call to turn. If you have read it right, an island rises out of the sea at first light.

**Keepsake and next purchase.** Your star path, the list of stars you steered by in order, and the name the crew gives you. Longer passages follow, each to a different island.

**Why it sells.** The ocean-voyaging fantasy behind a hit animated film franchise, safe for every age and worth replaying.

**Introduction (unchanged)**

> Thin gold light, and air so clean it feels sharpened. Your couch sits beside a road of fitted stone that climbs toward white peaks, and somebody is running up it. Wayra arrives hardly out of breath, a light-footed young man with knotted strings at his belt and a shell trumpet on his back. He crouches to your level. "You are not used to this height. **Pitch.** Run the Inca royal road as a relay messenger on the way to Machu Picchu: learn the message by heart, cross a gorge on a bridge woven of grass, and hand it on before the sun touches the peaks.

**What the player does.** Wayra runs his stretch of road whenever a message comes, and today the next man is slow, so the two of you carry it on together. **Why it sells.** Trekking the Inca Trail ranks second only to the northern lights on a global bucket-list survey, and a race against the sun needs no explaining.
